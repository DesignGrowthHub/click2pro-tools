What to Expect When Working With a Luxury Interior Designer

A step-by-step look at a full-service interior design project, from the first conversation and concept through procurement, installation and the reveal.

By Paul Lauren Designs · June 30, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • The best projects start with honest conversations about lifestyle, timeline and investment.
  • Expect a concept, then detailed design development, then procurement. Each stage builds on the last.
  • Ask who will actually design your home. At Paul Lauren Designs it is the principal, personally.

It begins with a conversation

Every project starts with listening: how you live, who lives with you, what you love and what you'd like to leave behind. We talk about scope, timing and budget openly, because a clear brief is the foundation of a beautiful, calm process.

Discovery, then concept

Next comes a visit to the home or a review of the plans, along with measuring, photographing and noting how light and views move through each space. From there the studio develops a concept that sets out the plan, palette, key materials and signature furnishings. It's presented in person, with real samples to touch, so decisions are made with confidence.

Design development: where the details live

Once the direction is set, every room is resolved in detail: finishes and fixtures, lighting, custom upholstery and case goods, fabrics, rugs, window treatments, art and accessories. On new construction and renovation this stage runs alongside the architect and builder so cabinetry, millwork and lighting are integrated rather than added on.

Procurement and project oversight

A full-service designer handles ordering, tracking, receiving and inspecting everything, often hundreds of items from many vendors with different lead times. This invisible work protects your time and your investment, and it's where experience shows most.

Installation and reveal

Ideally everything arrives at once. Furniture is placed, art is hung, beds are made and accessories are styled, and then the door opens on a finished home. For many clients it's the best day of the project.

One question worth asking

Ask any designer who will actually be making the design decisions for your home. At Paul Lauren Designs the answer is simple. As Lauren Rautbord puts it, the day she delegates design decisions or makes selections “off the shelf” is the day the firm has become too large.
